# balkhaev.com

A black hole and two ways to get in touch. Static HTML, CSS and TypeScript; no backend or runtime framework.

The renderer originated in the owner's Montage site at commit `184f50fc547d`. The page renders disk and stars in one Schwarzschild scene, framed by a quiet wordmark and two native contact links. Typography stays sharp and accessible above the canvas, independent of gravitational lensing. Disk interactions are sampled in the scene's material coordinates through the same curved rays as the image.

## Scene and interaction

The initial observer is 12.5 horizon radii from the centre, six degrees above the accretion disk plane. The lens has a fixed 64-degree field of view on the shorter screen dimension. Drag (0.15 degrees per CSS pixel) or use arrow keys (4 degrees per press) to look freely through 360 degrees; the mouse changes orientation, not the position of the black hole. Scroll down, spread two fingers, or press + to continue a single journey through the black hole, a speculative interior passage and the white-hole exterior. Scroll up, pinch or - rewinds the approach outside the horizon. Once crossed, the journey is forward-only; there is no restart button or reset shortcut. Tab reaches the always-visible Telegram and email links; focusing a link never changes the camera. Enter opens it.

The first scroll starts a radial free-fall sequence. Scroll controls progress along that worldline; a slow forward playback continues between gestures. Large scroll jumps are limited after horizon crossing so they cannot skip the interior and exit phases. The classical segment advances world time by the observer's proper-time increment. At r = 0.2 r_s it joins a continuous visual clock for the speculative passage. There is no end-of-trajectory screen: the observer emerges into a second star field and keeps travelling outward. A gentle cinematic turn reveals the receding white source while mouse look remains additive. Reduced motion disables autonomous travel and the automatic turn, while explicit flight and look controls still work. The idle opening samples successive rain observers at the same location until a flight begins.

## Relativistic rendering

Moving the mouse over the disk deposits bounded local pressure packets; tapping creates a stronger packet. Dragging continues to control the camera. Each contact uses the first visible disk intersection and its emission timestamp. Compression heats the bow edge while a depleted groove remains behind it. Packets co-rotate at each annulus's Keplerian rate, shear into spirals, propagate with a prescribed group speed below 0.26 c in material coordinates and damp away. The same emission field is visible in primary and delayed secondary images, rather than copied onto the screen. The 16-contact history is cleared on page reload or reversing world time. This is an interactive prescribed-emission approximation, not a GRMHD fluid simulation; the cursor acts as an external authoring input, not an in-world object moving between contacts. Fine thermal eddies and a periodically heated coherent vortex evolve with the disk, and the nearby star has prescribed photospheric granulation sampled at retarded emission time.

The renderer uses ingoing Painleve–Gullstrand coordinates, regular across the future event horizon, with metric ds² = -dT² + (dr + sqrt(1/r) dT)² + r² dΩ² in r_s = c = 1 units. The observer follows dr/dτ = -1/sqrt(r), dT/dτ = 1. Past-directed photons start in that observer's local orthonormal frame. Their conserved energy and angular momentum initialize u'' = -u + 3u²/2, with a rationalized PG travel-time equation that remains finite at the horizon for light arriving from outside.

The ray table is rebuilt for the actual camera radius. Rows cover the entire local sky with more samples near the escape-cone boundary; each row's angular samples span its own path length. The same curved rays intersect the disk and moving stellar photosphere in ray order. They provide delayed emission times, multiple images, capture and a lensed distant star field on both sides of the horizon. Frequency shifts use the photon covector contracted with each emitter's velocity and the observer's local frequency. The sky now uses thermal spectra rather than fixed RGB tints: for an emitter at infinity, g = 1 / (1 + n_r / sqrt(r)), including both gravitational and velocity shifts. A 1,024-sample logarithmic temperature table supplies Planck radiance for the disk, nearby star and sky. Temperature shifting includes spectral beaming once, without an additional g³ brightness multiplier. A faint procedural stellar band with grey dust absorption is anchored at infinity and sampled with the same escaped rays. Its curved and repeated images expose the changing aberration and lensing during infall. Point stars use the ratio of camera/emitter solid angles for magnification, capped at 12 to regularize unresolved caustics; the extended band preserves surface brightness apart from its spectral shift. There is no artificial fade, clipping plane or replacement sky at r = 1.

The disk spans radii 3–11 and uses a zero-torque Schwarzschild thin-disk temperature profile peaking at 3,200 K. Circular tracers and advected filaments modulate opacity. A compact 11,800 K star follows a bound eccentric geodesic between radii 12 and 32, with a prescribed coordinate photosphere radius of 0.34. Darwin's anomaly is integrated in PG time; the accumulated azimuth preserves relativistic precession between radial periods. A narrow optical point-spread function redistributes 1.8% of image energy over normalized blur kernels, concentrated at the smallest scale. Luminance tone mapping with hue-preserving gamut compression keeps bright thermal filaments from washing out into uniform white. The density combines advected filaments and fine granulation, using an integer hash and derivative-based filtering to suppress only detail below a pixel. Exposure and a sparse background star field establish contrast with the blue stellar photosphere.

This is a test-body, prescribed-emission model. It omits black-hole rotation, self-gravity, plasma dynamics, structural stresses, tidal deformation and radiative feedback. The compact star's coordinate sphere and limb-darkening law are approximations. Finite ray/orbit tables, retained image count, three spectral samples, a 400–200,000 K temperature lookup, filtering and display tone mapping introduce numerical approximations. The distant stellar band is a prescribed illustration, not a surveyed Milky Way map. The singularity itself is outside the model.

The table approach follows [Eric Bruneton](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08735). The falling frame and horizon-regular coordinates follow the [river model of black holes](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0411060). Eccentric orbit equations use the Darwin parameterization in [Hopper, Kavanagh and Ottewill, equations 2.5–2.6](https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevD.93.044010), transformed to PG time.

## Speculative passage and white-hole exit

The black-to-white continuation is an artistic interpretation inspired by hypothetical tunnelling scenarios such as [Haggard and Rovelli](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.0989), not a numerical solution of that paper or a confirmed traversable route. The classical Schwarzschild renderer is blended into the authored passage only inside the horizon. World-space cylindrical emission sheets surround the observer, with curved fibres, subluminal travelling pulses and delayed surface emission. A radiating aperture grows by perspective as the camera approaches it. The exterior has a resolved white source, an optically thin rotating launch pattern that becomes outward spiral filaments, and transparent radially emitted packets moving at 0.52 c. Wind sampling and packet intersections use emission time, and their spectra include the emitter's motion. The new distant sky uses special-relativistic aberration and a frequency shift for a prescribed outward velocity of 0.45 c. This authored exterior approximates flat-space transport; it does not implement the outgoing white-hole metric or claim an observed white-hole appearance. The bridge geometry, emission budget, phase durations and camera path are visual choices. The route remains continuous and does not return to the original landing view.

## Development

```sh
bun install
bun run dev
```

Open `http://localhost:4175`. Rebuild with `bun run build` after edits. Loopback-only `?preview-stage=4.7` holds a deterministic interior frame; `?preview-stage=8.5` previews the exterior. These parameters are ignored on production hosts. `bun run check` runs TypeScript and Ultracite; `bun test` checks light deflection/capture, null momentum, horizon continuity, interior light travel time against independent radial quadrature, timelike flight, one-way journey controls, passage/clock/camera continuity, stage visibility under large scroll gestures, orbit invariants, camera bases, spectral beaming invariance, sky shifts against independent Lorentz/gravity factors, spectral-table accuracy, adaptive quality and native-resolution budgets for Full HD, Retina mobile and 4K.

Animation pauses in a hidden tab and respects reduced motion. The contact links and a static fallback remain available without WebGL.

The renderer adapts its pixel budget (2.07 / 4.15 / 8.29 megapixels), bloom levels (2 / 3 / 4) and tracer count (4,500 / 12,000 / 28,000) to GPU performance. It renders at device-pixel resolution up to 3× before applying the budget, retaining native Full HD even in the lowest tier. Larger displays can still downscale when the active GPU budget is exceeded. Reduced-motion mode holds the simulation at a fixed time while allowing explicit camera navigation. Hidden tabs pause the display clock. Native contact anchors remain visible and keyboard/screen-reader accessible during flight and when WebGL is unavailable or lost. Status announcements occur only when the flight stage changes.

After committing and pushing, run `bun run deploy` with `COOLIFY_ACCESS_TOKEN` set and SSH key access to `root@49.13.216.63`. Deployment builds the committed revision on the server, tags the image with the commit for rollback, and deploys that image. No credentials are stored in this repository.
