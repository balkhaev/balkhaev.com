# balkhaev.com

A black hole and two ways to get in touch. Static HTML, CSS and TypeScript; no backend or runtime framework.

The renderer originated in the owner's Montage site at commit `184f50fc547d`. The page renders disk and stars in one Schwarzschild scene, framed by a quiet wordmark and two native contact links. Typography stays sharp and accessible above the canvas, independent of gravitational lensing. Disk interactions are sampled in the scene's material coordinates through the same curved rays as the image.

## Scene and interaction

The initial observer frame is 12.5 horizon radii from the centre, six degrees above the accretion disk plane. The initial rectilinear lens has a 64-degree field of view on the shorter screen dimension. Between radii 3 and 0.6 it smoothly changes to a 180-degree stereographic panoramic lens, exposing the peripheral and part of the rear hemisphere on a flat display. CPU picking and GPU rendering use identical unit sightlines. Guided gaze begins turning at logarithmic phase 1.65, about radius 2.4, smoothly reaching a visible external-sky ray with frequency ratio g = 2 at the horizon. Inside it follows an escaping sky direction with `g = 2/sqrt(r)`, approaching about 14.14 at the model boundary; the target yaw changes from 60 degrees at the horizon to about 82.45 degrees, with zero pitch. The lens and gaze are presentation choices; each physical radius samples a local radial rain-observer frame. Dragging (0.15 degrees per CSS pixel) or using arrow keys (4 degrees per press) immediately takes over the gaze for free 360-degree look. Guidance stays off until the visitor explicitly enables the gaze button again.

The scene opens at a held depth while the disk keeps rotating. Scroll down, spread two fingers, or press + to select a deeper observer radius. Scroll up, pinch or - selects an earlier depth, including after the horizon and from the final depth. Either direction cancels queued opposite motion, smoothly reaches the selected depth and holds there until the next input. The start button or Space explicitly starts or pauses continuous cinematic depth navigation. Pausing clears queued travel and holds the radius while source animation continues. This interaction explores local falling-observer frames; holding a radius or navigating back does not describe one visitor's physical worldline or a hovering observer inside the horizon. There is no restart button or automatic reset. Tab reaches the native contact links and scene controls; F toggles fullscreen when supported, and M toggles the optional sound atmosphere.

Uninterrupted cinematic depth navigation takes about 53 seconds. Its display pace changes smoothly and slows around horizon crossing so the transition remains readable; manual forward and reverse gestures have bounded rates. One shared PG scene epoch starts at zero and advances monotonically by `CLOCK_RATE = 3.8` units per active display second, independently of radius, navigation direction and playback state. The runtime increment is scene time, not proper time accumulated along the interactive route. Every source image is sampled at its own retarded emission epoch. Changing radius changes a ray's light-travel delay and can expose an older image of a source, while the shared source clock continues forwards. Hidden tabs, reduced motion and deterministic still previews freeze automatic scene-time updates.

The route and optical model end at radius 0.02: `END_PROGRESS = MODEL_END_PROGRESS = log(12.5 / 0.02)`. `journey.clock` is reference rain-geodesic metadata, bounded by `END_TIME = 2/3 × (12.5^1.5 − 0.02^1.5)`; it can decrease when selecting an earlier radius and is never used to rewind the runtime scene epoch. At the endpoint, `journey.completion` is 1 and radius holds at 0.02 while the live scene epoch continues forwards. The singularity lies outside the resolved model.

Quiet Russian chapters mark «Погружение», «Под внутренним краем», «Сфера света», «Перед горизонтом», «За горизонтом событий» and «Внешнее небо», ending at «Граница расчёта». The same curved rays, thermal emitters and distant-sky catalogue remain the visual source throughout the route. The final depth holds with live source animation, free look and outward radius navigation still available; there is no automatic reset. Reduced motion freezes automatic travel and source animation; the travel button becomes an explicit next-frame control, while gestures and free look remain available. The interaction concept is in [docs/experience.md](docs/experience.md).

Sound is off by default. The sound button or M creates a Web Audio graph only after a visitor gesture, with quiet synthesized tones and filtered noise shaped by radius and gaze. Sustained low tones, slow native modulation and damped stereo echoes remain active at a held depth; pause and the physical endpoint soften the atmosphere. Hidden tabs mute and suspend the audio context. This is a procedural soundtrack rather than a model of sound propagation, and it requires no audio downloads.

## Relativistic rendering

Moving the mouse over the disk deposits bounded local pressure packets; tapping creates a stronger radial splash. Dragging continues to control the camera. Each contact uses the first visible disk intersection and its emission timestamp. Adjacent contacts form a swept cut measured relative to the orbiting gas, with proper radial distances and wrapped azimuths. Compression heats a corrugated bow edge and a secondary ripple, while a depleted groove and two counter-rotating eddies bend the material behind it. Packets co-rotate at each annulus's Keplerian rate, shear into spirals, propagate at a prescribed 0.20 c in local material coordinates and damp away. Each contact also launches three heated fragments at 0.10–0.18 c relative to the material, with short cooling trails. Circular tracers use narrow orbital filaments with resolved bright heads; fragment emission and tracer deformation are sampled in the disk through the curved rays, preserving lensing and delayed secondary images. Smooth saturation bounds overlapping density and heating. A bounded archive retains 1,024 authored contacts until page reload. Each disk image selects up to 24 packets at its own retarded emission time from a sorted floating-point texture, with a 42-unit emission lifetime. Returning to an earlier depth preserves source events; each new ray independently selects their visible emission epoch. The global source clock keeps advancing, so the same radius need not restore the same optical frame. Distant jumps, pauses and clicks also break a continuous cut. This is an interactive prescribed-emission approximation, not a GRMHD fluid simulation; the cursor acts as an external authoring input, not an in-world object moving between contacts. Fine thermal eddies and a periodically heated coherent vortex evolve with the disk, and the nearby star has prescribed photospheric granulation sampled at retarded emission time.

The renderer uses ingoing Painleve–Gullstrand coordinates, regular across the future event horizon, with metric ds² = -dT² + (dr + sqrt(1/r) dT)² + r² dΩ² in r_s = c = 1 units. Its local observer tetrad is that of radial rain infall: a reference geodesic obeys dr/dτ = -1/sqrt(r), dT/dτ = 1. Past-directed photons start in this local orthonormal frame at the selected radius and live scene epoch. Interactive radius navigation is independent of that reference geodesic's wristwatch. Their conserved energy and angular momentum initialize u'' = -u + 3u²/2, with a rationalized PG travel-time equation that remains finite at the horizon for light arriving from outside.

The ray table is rebuilt for the actual camera radius. Rows cover the entire local sky with more samples near the escape-cone boundary; each row’s angular samples span its own path length and cluster at both ends. Adaptive radial steps remain accurate at small observer radii. The travel-time texture stores the residual after subtracting the analytic radial incoming-light clock r - 2 sqrt(r) + 2 log(1 + sqrt(r)); reconstructing this term at the actual interpolated radius preserves far-emitter timing. Stellar ray tangents and clock derivatives use the null first integral rather than fixed finite differences that could cross the endpoint. The same curved rays intersect the disk and moving stellar photosphere in ray order. They provide delayed emission times, multiple images, capture and a lensed distant star field on both sides of the horizon. Frequency shifts use the photon covector contracted with each emitter's velocity and the observer's local frequency. The sky now uses thermal spectra rather than fixed RGB tints: for an emitter at infinity, g = 1 / (1 + n_r / sqrt(r)), including both gravitational and velocity shifts. A 2,048-sample logarithmic temperature table supplies Planck radiance for the disk, nearby star and sky. Temperature shifting includes spectral beaming once, without an additional g³ brightness multiplier. A faint procedural stellar band with grey dust absorption is anchored at infinity and sampled with the same escaped rays. Its curved and repeated images expose the changing aberration and lensing during infall. Point stars use the ratio of camera/emitter solid angles for magnification, capped at 12 to regularize unresolved caustics; the extended band preserves surface brightness apart from its spectral shift. There is no artificial fade, clipping plane or replacement sky at r = 1.

The distant sky has a fixed catalogue of 1,581 point sources, with world directions sampled according to solid angle and temperatures from 3,200 to 14,000 K. A separate instanced pass inverts the same escape-angle table to locate each source's direct, opposite-parity and retained winding images. This avoids losing a source when a pixel spans many sky cells near the compressed interior band. The analytic Jacobian of the actual table interpolation sets magnification; the camera lens's solid-angle Jacobian converts integrated source flux to pixel radiance. A half-pixel Gaussian aperture response is integrated across each pixel, preserving subpixel flux during motion instead of sampling a point at the pixel centre. Disk and photosphere transmission attenuate these images in the main scene pass. The extended band and dust lane use a Gaussian approximation to the pixel footprint, with gradients computed before divergent ray branches, to reduce narrow-band aliasing. Sources remain unresolved points; no artificial angular size stretches them into arcs. The finite image count, interpolation and magnification cap remain model approximations.

Ten additional variable test emitters occupy fixed world directions on a finite sphere at radius 60. They are idealized supported static emitters, with prescribed smooth luminosity cycles of 9–19 `r_s/c` in their own proper time and a small thermal modulation. The two peaks within each authored cycle are distinct from the delayed copies produced by lensing. The finite-radius map solves each retained direct, opposite-parity and winding path's angle and PG travel delay; all images of a source sample one underlying history at `τem = sqrt(1 − 1/60) × (Tobs − delay)`, with a fixed common choice of epoch origin. Its frequency ratio includes the source's static lapse, and its magnification uses the finite map's angular Jacobian with the same point response and foreground transmission. Different paths reveal different moments of one source; no global clock is sped up to animate them. The luminosity curves are prescribed examples, not a calculation of pulsar or stellar dynamics. The steady catalogue at infinity remains unchanged.

The circular disk spans radii 3–11 and uses a zero-torque Schwarzschild thin-disk temperature profile peaking at 2,800 K, with local prescribed heating. A finite, inhomogeneous 2,050 K emission boundary blends into the inner disk over radii 3–3.8 and feeds a plunging flow extending down to radius 0.02. This additional prescribed boundary emission is not a zero-stress thermodynamic solution. Circular tracers, advected filaments and resolved narrow strands modulate opacity and emissivity. A compact 11,800 K star follows a bound eccentric geodesic between radii 12 and 32, with a prescribed coordinate photosphere radius of 0.34. Darwin's anomaly is integrated in PG time; the accumulated azimuth preserves relativistic precession between radial periods. A narrow optical point-spread function redistributes 1.8% of image energy over normalized blur kernels, concentrated at the smallest scale. Luminance tone mapping with hue-preserving gamut compression retains thermal colour. The density combines advected filaments and fine granulation, using an integer hash and derivative-based filtering to suppress only detail below a pixel. Global photographic exposure is metered from weighted logarithmic scene luminance in a 64×64 GPU pass reduced to one pixel, with exponential adaptation in display time. One exposure applies to all pixels and follows scene light rather than flight stage. The narrow optical response and spectral tone mapping retain the contrast between the disk, dark gaps and the blue stellar photosphere.

Inside the ISCO, flow elements follow equatorial timelike geodesics with L = sqrt(3), E = sqrt(8/9 + 0.045²) and an inward launch four-velocity of -0.045 at r = 3. A 2,048-sample logarithmic radius table stores their PG age, accumulated azimuth and proper age. Ages are stored relative to a shared clock so tiny interior travel-time differences retain floating-point precision. Injection time and angle advect the same irregular material features into the plunge; the mean surface density obeys r Sigma |U^r| = constant. The emitter's PG four-velocity and photon frequency contraction are rationalized across r = 1; the GPU uses the equivalent local rain-frame Lorentz contraction with a stable 1-beta term. Interior emission is composited through the same past lightcone, including negative Killing-energy photons that can originate inside. Density, thermal emission and opacity remain prescribed approximations. The kinematic approach follows [Mummery & Balbus, ISCO inspirals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03579) and the material-advection picture follows [Mummery & Stone, plunging-region spiral structure](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/532/3/3395/7705619); this implementation does not reproduce their full thermodynamic or GRMHD models.

This is a test-body, prescribed-emission model. It omits black-hole rotation, self-gravity, plasma dynamics, structural stresses, tidal deformation and radiative feedback. The compact star's coordinate sphere and limb-darkening law are approximations. Finite ray/orbit tables, retained image count, three spectral samples, a 400–20,000,000 K temperature lookup, filtering and display tone mapping introduce numerical approximations. The distant stellar band, its resolved bulge, young patches and grey dust absorption are a prescribed illustration fixed in world directions, not a surveyed Milky Way map. The singularity itself is outside the model.

The physical interior follows the same past lightcone: there is no visual discontinuity at the true horizon and the model contains no luminous singularity. Multiple images of rear sources can appear in the forward hemisphere near the shadow edge. Deeper in, the external sky is increasingly concentrated towards directions transverse to radial infall, while the directly rearward sky redshifts. This behavior follows [Hamilton and Polhemus, The edge of locality](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.4717) and [Hamilton’s Schwarzschild visualization](https://jila.colorado.edu/~ajsh/insidebh/schw.html).

The table approach follows [Eric Bruneton](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08735). The falling frame and horizon-regular coordinates follow the [river model of black holes](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0411060). Eccentric orbit equations use the Darwin parameterization in [Hopper, Kavanagh and Ottewill, equations 2.5–2.6](https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevD.93.044010), transformed to PG time.

## Physical diagnostics

The optional «Физика» panel reports the selected local rain frame and the live PG scene clock. Its central distant-sky value is `g = νobs/ν∞` only when the central sightline reaches infinity; captured directions are marked as having no external source. The centre, transverse 90° and rearward 180° samples distinguish directions without a distant source. Received ticks from a distant clock have this instantaneous rate relative to the local rain observer along the relevant light ray. The rate varies with direction: at radius 0.02 the guided ray approaches 14.14, the transverse ray gives 1 and the rearward ray about 0.124. A brighter or faster signal in one direction does not imply that the entire external universe speeds up uniformly. These ratios refer to the emitter and observer's proper intervals along a ray; neither the global PG epoch nor `journey.clock` is multiplied by g.

The panel's small clock comparison integrates a local reference at 1× alongside the received centre and rearward rates. It illustrates the current tangent-frame comparison while holding its geometry; it is not a calendar of the sources' distant future or a reconstruction of the visitor's full worldline. A held navigation radius samples a family of local falling frames at successive PG epochs, so the temporal derivative of the displayed source light curve need not equal the instantaneous rain-frame g. The actual rendered light curves use their source emission epochs and the calculated travel delay; the clock illustration is separate from that source history.

The panel also shows the rain observer's speed relative to a static observer, the static clock factor `sqrt(1 − 1/r)`, tidal strength relative to the initial radius 12.5, and the visible shadow diameter, whose boundary separates the distant-sky cone from captured directions. Static speed and static clock comparisons are available only at `r > 1`. Tidal strength is the relative gradient `(12.5/r)^3`; a black-hole mass and physical length scale are needed to convert it to an acceleration across a body.

«Карта частот» applies false colour to the actual shader frequency ratio `g = νobs/νem` of each contributing disk, plunging-plasma, stellar, finite external or distant-sky source. Its logarithmic legend marks 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4; stronger shifts approach the palette's red or blue limits. Diagnostic brightness keeps very redshifted sources inspectable and intentionally differs from optical surface brightness. Multiple sources still composite in ray order with their respective diagnostic brightness and transmission; a mixed pixel is not a single emitter's measured g. The panel's distant-sky ratios refer to emitters at infinity; the finite variable sources use their own emitter lapse in the rendering. Diagnostics remain available at the final resolved radius. The local-frame distinction follows the [river model](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0411060); horizon continuity and the visible external sky are illustrated by [Hamilton's Schwarzschild journey](https://jila.colorado.edu/~ajsh/insidebh/schw.html).

```sh
bun install
bun run dev
```

Values beyond `END_PROGRESS` clamp to that endpoint. Sources keep animating and travel controls remain available. Add `&preview-still=1` to freeze time for deterministic visual QA. Only in that still mode, add `&preview-effect=stream` or `&preview-effect=impact` and `&preview-age=3` to compare disk responses at a given age. These parameters are ignored on production hosts. `bun run check` runs TypeScript and Ultracite; `bun test` checks light deflection/capture, null momentum, horizon continuity, interior light travel time against independent radial quadrature, reference timelike rain infall, bidirectional radius navigation, a monotonic source epoch across depth gestures and playback states, cancellation of queued opposite gestures, playback start/pause/resume, reduced-motion control, refresh-rate-independent pacing, bounded transitions, final-frame navigation, reference proper-time continuity and its fixed model endpoint, orbit invariants, camera bases, guided gaze and manual takeover, spectral beaming invariance, sky shifts against independent Lorentz/gravity factors, spectral-table accuracy, material-relative cut continuity, bounded fragment speeds and history, adaptive quality and native-resolution budgets for Full HD, Retina mobile and 4K. Additional tests check the bounded emission archive and source-event selection at retarded epochs, plunging-flow timelike normalization and Killing invariants through the horizon, ages and winding against independent quadrature, frequency shifts against an independent rain-frame Lorentz contraction, advected injection labels and mean mass flux. Audio tests cover a quiet physical endpoint, retained sound at held depths, explicit user enablement, finite parameters and browser lifecycle safety.

Sky-specific tests additionally check fixed unit source directions, total spherical area, inverse image positions against independent null-first-integral quadrature, opposite image parity, rear sources appearing ahead and magnification against a finite-difference Jacobian. Variable-source tests check proper periods, positive smooth light curves through negative emission epochs, and one shared history for differently delayed images. Local clock comparisons preserve their illustrative phase through depth and gaze changes and agree across frame rates without altering the source epoch. Animation pauses in a hidden tab and respects reduced motion. The contact links and a static fallback remain available without WebGL.

The display loop is capped at 60 fps, including the idle scene, and pauses in hidden tabs. Stale returning frames cannot skip forward through flight chapters. The renderer adapts its pixel budget (2.07 / 4.15 / 8.29 megapixels), bloom levels (2 / 3 / 4) and tracer count (4,500 / 12,000 / 28,000) to GPU performance. It renders at device-pixel resolution up to 3× before applying the budget, retaining native Full HD even in the lowest tier. Larger displays can still downscale when the active GPU budget is exceeded. Reduced-motion mode holds simulation time between explicit travel inputs and allows explicit camera navigation. Native contact anchors remain visible and keyboard/screen-reader accessible during flight and when WebGL is unavailable or lost. Status announcements occur only when the chapter or playback state changes; the radius and progress indicators are decorative.

## Production

Coolify serves an Nginx image on `https://balkhaev.com`. DNS points to `49.13.216.63`. The Docker image listens on port 8080; TLS is handled by Coolify.

After committing and pushing, run `bun run deploy` with `COOLIFY_ACCESS_TOKEN` set and SSH key access to `root@49.13.216.63`. Deployment builds the committed revision on the server, tags the image with the commit for rollback, and deploys that image. No credentials are stored in this repository.

After publishing, check the production URL and container health, then verify the live scene in a browser. At the initial radius and a held interior radius, wait without scrolling: source luminosity, disk features and the PG clock should advance while radius holds. Move down and back up: the PG clock should remain monotonic, while different retarded rays can show different emission ages. Check «Физика» and «Карта частот» outside and across the horizon, including unavailable static comparisons and central rays without an external source. Compare the centre and rearward signals in the optional local illustration; turning or changing depth should change their rates without resetting their phases or rescaling PG time. Repeated images of a variable source should retain the same underlying pattern at different delays. At the final radius 0.02, confirm «Граница расчёта», live source animation, available diagnostics and outward depth navigation. Hidden-tab return should cause no time jump; reduced motion, touch/keyboard navigation, contacts and opt-in audio should remain available.
